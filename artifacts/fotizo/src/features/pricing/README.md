# Fotizo rates — frontend and backend handoff

`rates.json` is the approved rate schedule. The seller dashboard displays it; the Alibaba import tools use its Chinese-goods markup. This change does not debit accounts, charge cards, deduct seller payouts or write fees to a database.

- Artisans/service providers: charge GH₵1, US$1, £1 or €1 per paid booking through Fotizo, including every repeat booking by the same customer. Deduplicate by booking ID across devices, payment retries and webhook deliveries so each booking is charged once. A new booking creates a new fee even when the provider and customer are unchanged. Apply only after confirmed payment, never on a lead or profile view.
- Sellers/shops: charge one unit of the transaction currency per product unit sold. For quantity three, the fee is GH₵3 / US$3 / £3 / €3. The seller bears the fee; do not add it as a buyer surcharge. Settlement and refund/reversal timing still need to be agreed before enabling collection.
- Chinese-source goods: supplier cost × 1.30. Applies to Alibaba, Taobao and Pinduoduo. This is 30% markup, not a 30% profit margin and not an extra 30% on top of a previously marked-up price. Freight, duties and final delivery costs are not established by this rule.

Flat fees are nominal amounts in the transaction's recorded currency, not GBP amounts converted through the buyer's display-currency switcher. EUR is included in the fee schedule; adding EUR to payments/display exchange rates requires separate currency support. Unsupported transaction currencies must not silently fall back to another currency.

Backend implementation needs authoritative transaction currencies, booking identity and provider/customer identity, atomic and idempotent ledger entries, quantity-aware seller fees and payout/refund accounting. Do not infer collection from a frontend button, status badge or client-supplied price. Whether Fotizo's own sourced shop should also incur the seller fee is unresolved; the frontend adds only the 30% markup to imported product prices.

The catalogue retains its existing fixed import USD/GBP conversion (1.27 USD per GBP) as an estimate. JSON-sourced listings are recalculated from stored supplier quotes; older inline Alibaba entries lack raw quotes and use the approximate base recovered from the old 2.2 multiplier. No stock quantities are added.

## Backend implementation (migration 0015)

- **Rates:** the server's copy is `FEE_SCHEDULE` / `CHINESE_GOODS` in `lib/db/src/schema/pricing.ts`; an API test fails if it drifts from `rates.json`.
- **Seller fees:** recorded when an order is paid (provider-confirmed Paystack/Stripe payment, or a manager recording an offline payment): one fee per order line, quantity × the flat amount in the currency actually charged (GH₵ for Paystack, £ for Stripe and pay on delivery). The seller bears it; buyer totals are unchanged. Cancelled lines are skipped, and Fotizo's own shop lines are skipped unless `FEES_ON_FOTIZO_SHOP=true` (pending decision).
- **Artisan fees:** £1 per booking, recorded when the provider marks the booking completed — bookings are not paid through Fotizo yet, so completion stands in for confirmed payment until booking payments exist. Repeat bookings are separate bookings and each is charged.
- **No double charges:** unique indexes allow one fee per booking ID and one per order line, so retried payment confirmations and repeated webhooks record nothing new. Unsupported currencies record no fee (logged) instead of falling back.
- **Reporting:** `GET /api/earnings` gives each seller/provider their gross earnings, fees and net payout per currency plus recent fee records (reference, account, currency, amount, status). The seller dashboard's Earnings tab shows it. Fees are `pending` — nothing is deducted until collection timing and reversals are agreed.
- **Imported pricing:** products store their source platform, supplier product ID and link, supplier currency and cost, the exchange rate used, markup and when they were priced. `pnpm --filter @workspace/scripts import-shop-catalogue -- --dry-run` imports `alibaba-products.json` and the remaining `products.ts` records, deduplicating by platform + supplier product ID and keeping existing IDs, images and status. Quoted goods are priced at the lowest supplier quote × 1.30 converted at the live rate; older listings without a quote keep their price with the cost recovered from it. Shipping is priced separately; stock quantities are not shown.
- **Taobao:** the import also reads `taobao-products.json` (CNY quotes, priced at quote × 1.30 converted at the live CNY/GBP rate) and skips any record `taobao-import-manifest.json` marks as anything other than a physical product (services, digital goods, deposits). New departments (Furniture, Office Supplies, Clocks, Smart Devices, Industrial Parts & Supplies) are added to the categories table. Taobao images keep their `/images/taobao/…` paths, served by the frontend.
- **Preview links keep working:** catalogue ids such as `ali-…`, `taobao-…` and `t-161` map deterministically to the stored UUIDs, so `GET /api/products/:id`, related products, carts (including merging a guest cart), wishlists and order items accept either form. Product URLs and saved references from the preview build stay valid after switching to the API catalogue.
- **Supplier terms in the API:** shop products return `sourcing` (platform, supplier product ID and link, currency, price range, minimum order, unit, capture date) with `priceStatus: "estimate"`, since supplier prices, variants, minimums and delivery still need confirming before purchase. Shop products report `inStock: true` because they are sourced to order; stock quantities are not shown.
- **Confirmation before payment:** an order containing imported goods (shop products with a supplier source) is placed with `confirmationStatus: "awaiting"` and is not charged. At checkout the buyer sees each item's supplier minimum and can say which options (colour, size, model) they want. The China representative or a manager reviews it under **Order confirmations** (`GET /operations/confirmations`) beside the supplier's price range, minimum order and listing, then either sends a quote (`POST /operations/orders/:id/quote`: confirmed GBP unit price and options per imported line, delivery fee, delivery time in days, optional note) or declines with a reason (`POST /operations/orders/:id/decline`). The buyer accepts the quote (`POST /orders/:id/accept`), which opens Paystack/Stripe for the confirmed total, or cancels (`POST /orders/:id/withdraw`). Quotes expire after 7 days. Payment, the 60-minute payment window, manager mark-paid and seller fulfilment all wait until the buyer accepts. Marketplace lines in the same order keep their price and their reserved stock is released if the order is declined, cancelled or expires. Fees are recorded on the confirmed prices once paid.
